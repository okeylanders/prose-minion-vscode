# Category: register shift (`register`)

Flag narration that leaves the register the chapter keeps: a few words whose
register the rest of the narration never uses. A chapter's register is its
habit of words: how formal or plain, how old or new, how close or cool, how
ornate or bare.

## First, the chapter's register

Before you flag anything, read the chapter's narration, never its dialogue,
and name the register it keeps in `register`, at the root of your reply
beside `flags`: one line, a phrase or two, such as plain, wry, unhurried
close third in an older English. Every flag is a shift from that line, so a
reply with flags always carries it.

- Name a period only when the narration itself sets one. You may name it by
  its years.
- The line follows the rules of a note, except that years are allowed.
- When there is nothing to flag, you may leave `register` out.

## The kinds

- `period`: a word or phrase from outside the period the narration sets.
- `slang`: casual or slang wording in narration that keeps a plainer or more formal register.
- `jargon`: technical, legal, clinical or corporate wording in narration that keeps none.
- `ornate`: a sudden heightened, ornate passage in narration that keeps a plain register.

Flag `period` only when the narration sets a period and your `register`
names it. Without one, a word is never flagged for its date. When words fit
more than one kind, name the one that says what they bring: a word that
dates the narration is `period`, however casual it is.

## What to leave alone

- **Dialogue.** A character's register is their voice. Never flag words
  inside quotation marks, double or single.
- **A character's own thought.** Where the text marks narration as a
  character's thought (thought Alice, a question she asks herself), her
  idiom is hers. When you cannot tell whether a line is her thought, flag it
  only if the shift is hard, and say in `why` that it may be her own.
- **A shift the chapter makes on purpose and keeps:** a letter or a document
  it quotes, a change of narrator at a scene break.
- **The narration's own habits.** A word the narration keeps using belongs
  to its register, and a word that is only uncommon is not a shift.

## The note

- `why`: the register the words bring, against the one the chapter keeps.
- `suggestion`: one way it could go in the chapter's own register.

## The reply's root

```
{"version":1,"register":"…","flags":[{"original":"…","before":"…","after":"…","kind":"…","why":"…","suggestion":"…"}]}
```
