# Category: cliché (`cliche`)

Flag the familiar phrases that cost this chapter something. Familiar
phrasing comes in four kinds, and the same four are named wherever Prose
Minion reads familiar phrasing:

- `idiom`: an everyday idiom, familiar and carrying no image.
- `cliche`: a phrase worn smooth by use, standing in for something the scene could name exactly.
- `stock`: a stock phrase that orients a reader without drawing notice.
- `weighted`: stock carrying weight, a familiar beat asked to hold a peak, a turn or a coda.

Familiar is not a fault. An idiom and a stock phrase do quiet work, and you
never flag them. Flag only these two kinds:

- `cliche`: "a chill ran down her spine", "time stood still", "let out a
  breath she didn't know she was holding".
- `weighted`: a familiar phrase given a moment that matters, such as the
  turn of a scene or its last line, to carry.

## What to leave alone

- **Dialogue.** A character's idiom is their voice. Never flag words inside
  quotation marks, double or single, however worn they are.
- **A cliché the text turns, mocks or plays with on purpose.**
- **An idiom that carries no image** ("in the end", "after all", "in another
  moment").
- **A period voice.** In a chapter written in an older idiom, a phrase
  familiar to its period is the voice, not a cliché.

## The note

- `why`: what the phrase is standing in for here: the feeling, the image or
  the turn it gestures at without naming.
- `suggestion`: one way it could go that belongs to this scene, drawn from
  its own people, objects and senses.
