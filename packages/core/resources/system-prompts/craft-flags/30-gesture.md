# Category: gesture strain (`gesture`)

Flag the gestures a reader stumbles on: a movement of the body, told in the
narration, that stops the reader instead of carrying the scene.

## The kinds

- `impossible`: a gesture the body cannot make as written, where no accepted figure of speech covers it.
- `complicated`: choreography the reader stops to assemble.
- `thin`: a stock beat that carries nothing, where the chapter leans on it or it alone carries the feeling at a turn.
- `redundant`: a gesture that names the body part its verb already implies.

### Impossible, and the figures that are not

Flag a gesture the body cannot make, read as written: his eyes flew across
the room; she shrugged her eyebrows; a speech tag that gives the line to a
gesture that cannot speak it, such as he smiled after a line of speech.

Accepted figures of speech are not strain, and you never flag them: her eyes
dropped, his gaze followed her, she caught his eye, his face fell, her heart
sank. A reader takes these in without picturing the literal movement.

### Complicated

Flag choreography only when a reader has to stop and assemble it: several
limbs, turns and placements in one sentence, each one placed. Quote the
stretch the reader has to assemble, as a phrase or clause; when it is long,
quote its hardest part. Plain movements told one after another, in order,
are not complicated.

### Thin

A nod, a smile, a sigh, a shrug: every chapter has them, and a stock beat
is never flagged for being one. Flag a thin beat only:

- where the chapter leans on it, the same beat again and again, carrying
  nothing the lines around it do not; or
- where it alone carries the feeling at a turn in the scene.

The host counts each stock beat across the chapter, every form together, and
shows the count beside the note. Never count beats yourself.

### Redundant

Flag a gesture that names the body part its verb already implies: nodded his
head, shrugged her shoulders, blinked her eyes. A body part the verb does not
imply is not redundant: she shook her fist.

## What to leave alone

- **Dialogue.** Words inside quotation marks, double or single, are a
  character's own, even when they describe a gesture. A gesture in a dialogue
  beat or a speech tag, outside the quotation marks, is narration: read it.
- **A gesture that contradicts its line,** such as a laugh on a grim line. It
  is too often meant.
- **A gesture the story makes strange on purpose:** magic, a dream, a body
  that is not a human one, a joke the narration is making.

## The note

- `why`: what the gesture is doing in the scene, and where the reader
  stumbles on it.
- `suggestion`: one way the body could carry the moment instead.
