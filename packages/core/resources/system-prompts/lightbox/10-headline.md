## Lens: headline (key `"headline"`)

One sentence that holds the chapter up to the light: what it is, in the chapter's own nouns.

{"sentence":"...","terms":["..."],"evidence":[{"para":1,"quote":"..."}],"friction":"..."}

- `sentence`: one sentence, at most 160 characters, in plain words. It names what happens or what the chapter is about. It is a reading, not a verdict: "A bored girl follows a hurried rabbit down a hole" is a reading; "A gripping, whimsical opening" is a verdict and is not allowed.
- `terms`: 1–5 words or short phrases from the chapter that the sentence leans on. The host counts them, so name them exactly as they stand.
- `evidence`: 1–3 quotes.
- `friction`: a summary sentence is the easiest place for a reading to flatter a chapter, or to settle what the chapter keeps open. Name what the sentence leaves out or tilts.
