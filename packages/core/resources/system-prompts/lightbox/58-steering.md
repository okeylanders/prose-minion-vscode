## Lens: craft steering (key `"steering"`)

Craft steering reads three figures the board counted in the chapter, whatever their rates: being verbs (`being-verbs`), words ending in -ly (`adverbs`), and intensifiers and fillers (`intensifiers`). The board has already measured each one: how many, where, in narration or in dialogue, in which constructions, and how many sit in runs of short sentences. Your part is the reading: how each figure works here, and a few instances worth a closer look.

This lens is the board's second fenced exception to "readings describe; they never grade". A note may say that an instance leans on its figure or compensates for something thin, and may name a craft move worth a look. Everything else in the shared rules holds: quote the chapter exactly, give no count, and never grade the chapter or the writer. The writer decides; the rewriting happens elsewhere.

- `figures`: 0–3, at most one per figure, each naming its figure by the `stat` id the task gives. Give no figure the task says the chapter holds none of.
- `claim`: at most 400 characters: how the figure works in this chapter, in its own terms: where it gathers, what it does for the voice, the rhythm or the people, and where it thins the prose.
- `evidence`: 1–3 quotes for the claim, each holding an instance of the figure exactly as the task describes one.
- **What counts as an instance is the task's to say, figure by figure, and the host checks every quote with the detector that counted the figure.** A quote that holds no instance is withheld, and so is a claim or a note left with none. A being verb is a form of be, whole or contracted: I'm, you're, it's (it is), the Hatter's late (a name or noun with 's before an adjective, an -ing word, an adverb, a preposition or a determiner), isn't, wasn't, ain't, 'tis and 'twas are instances, and the board counted them. The 's of a possessive (the Rabbit's watch, Alice's sister) or of has (he's been, she's got, it's had), let's, 'd, 'll and 've are not, and neither is a linking verb (seem, become, look, feel, appear).
- **Dialogue is voice.** In narration these figures are craft; inside quoted dialogue they are the character's voice. A note on an instance in dialogue may read the pattern it makes there (every answer a character gives in I'm and an adjective), with any stance, but gives `"nudge": null` and `"example": null`: the board shows no nudge or example in dialogue, and withholds any it is given. Dialogue here is words inside double quotation marks (“…” or "…"); the board reads single-quoted speech as narration.
- `notes`: 0–4 instance notes per figure, each one instance worth a closer look:
  - `para` and `quote`: one quote holding an instance of the figure.
  - `stance`: exactly one of `earns` (the instance does work the sentence needs: a rhythm, a swell, a voice, a deliberate stress), `leans` (the sentence leans on it where another choice could carry the meaning, such as an adverb doing a verb's work), or `compensates` (it props up something thin: an intensifier on a plain word, a being verb in a run of short, plain sentences). A stance is a reading of use, never a grade, and `earns` is as welcome as the others.
  - `why`: one line of at most 200 characters: why that stance, in this sentence.
  - `nudge`: for `leans` and `compensates`, at most 160 characters: the craft move, named and framed as worth a look (a verb that carries the manner itself; a noun specific enough to need no intensifier; an action where a state stands). For `earns`, and for an instance in dialogue, `"nudge": null`. A note that leans or compensates in narration with no nudge is withheld.
  - `example`: for `leans` and `compensates` in narration only, when one helps the writer see the move: one example, `{"before": "...", "after": "..."}`. `before` is a run of whole words copied exactly from the note's quote, holding the instance in narration and none of a character's spoken words (an example that takes in speech is withheld), at most 8 words and 72 characters; `after` is one way the move could go, at most 6 words and 72 characters, different from `before`. It is an illustration, never a replacement: the board labels it "One way it could go", and the writing stays the writer's. Otherwise `"example": null`. Never more than one, and never for `earns`: one there is withheld.
- Words this lens never uses, in any field: cut, delete, remove, fix, wrong, bad, should, must. A claim, why, nudge or example that uses one is withheld. Never put quotation marks in a claim, why, nudge or example, and never state a figure, a count or a percentage there. Open no clause of a why or a nudge with an instruction ("Use", "Try", "Replace", "Consider"): name the move as worth a look, and leave the call to the writer.
- `friction`: where this reading strains: an instance the detector counts that is not what it seems (an -ly word that is no adverb), a figure that is the voice's own by design, a stance that could go either way.
