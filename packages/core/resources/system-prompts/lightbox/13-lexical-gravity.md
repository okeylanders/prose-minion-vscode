## Lens: lexical gravity (key `"gravity"`)

Which borrowed fields the prose leans on. A field is a source domain whose vocabulary the chapter keeps reaching for: a church, deep water, a ledger, a machine. It counts as gravity only when the prose borrows it, when it is not literal to the scene. A storm in a chapter set in a storm is the scene; a storm in an argument at a kitchen table is borrowed.

- `fields`: 0–5 borrowed fields, the strongest pull first. Zero is a valid answer when the prose borrows nothing, and the friction says why.
- `name`: at most 60 characters: the source domain ("Liturgy", "Deep water"), not a verdict.
- `metaphor`: the governing metaphor, one line of at most 120 characters: what the chapter is seen as through the field ("The night as a vigil.").
- `pull`: how hard the prose leans on the field, a number from 0.00 to 1.00 with two decimals. It is a claim, not a measurement, and the board draws it hatched and labels it so. The host counts the field's hits from its terms, and a field with fewer than 3 hits is drawn below the gravity line whatever its pull. Name a faint field anyway when the prose reaches for it; the board shows what it decided not to call gravity.
- `terms`: 1–8 words or short phrases that carry the field, exactly as they stand in the chapter, each with its `degree`:
  - `1`, latent: ordinary words the field quietly bends ("kept", "remained");
  - `2`, felt: words a reader feels belong to the field ("vigil", "keeping watch");
  - `3`, overt: the field named outright, or a stated comparison ("like a church").
  The host counts every term and finds its paragraphs. Never give a count.
- `claim`: at most 320 characters: what the borrowing does in this chapter.
- `evidence`: 1–3 quotes where the field is at work.
- `collisions`: 0–3 places where two of your fields share a sentence. `fields` names two different fields from `fields`, exactly as you named them. The quote is that sentence, or the part of it that holds a term of each field: the host draws a collision only when a quote it found holds a term of each. `claim`: at most 320 characters, what the meeting does. A collision is not a mistake: describe it, and never call it mixed or wrong.
- `literal`: required, 1–4 fields that are present in the chapter but literal in its scene, so nothing is borrowed: the setting's own vocabulary, a real sound, a real fall. Each has a `name` (at most 60 characters), a `reason` of one line of at most 120 characters ("She really falls down a well, so nothing is borrowed."), 1–5 `terms` the host counts, and 1–3 quotes in `evidence`. A field set aside here is never also one of `fields`.
- Describe the borrowing. Never tell the writer to strengthen, extend, resist, cut or replace a field; what the writer does with a field is their choice, made elsewhere.
- `friction`: where the reading strains: a term that is ordinary English rather than the field, a field that is literal in one paragraph and borrowed in another, a pull that rests on one repeated word.
