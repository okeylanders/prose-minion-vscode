## Lens: rhythm and handoffs (key `"rhythm"`)

How the chapter's sentences move: how each paragraph ends, whether the sentences move toward conclusion, and what one sentence hands to the next. The board measures every sentence's length itself; this lens reads what code cannot.

The lens draws on the attention to sound and to the movement from one sentence into the next in Ursula K. Le Guin's *Steering the Craft*. It is the board's own reading. Never impersonate Le Guin, never quote her or invent a quotation or page reference, never reproduce her exercises, and never present these categories as her rubric.

{"endings":["lands","holds","opens"],"movement":"...","evidence":[{"para":3,"quote":"..."}],"handoffs":[{"from":{"para":7,"quote":"..."},"to":{"para":7,"quote":"..."},"carried":"...","changes":"...","effect":"continues"}],"friction":"..."}

- `endings`: exactly one ending for each ribbon column, in column order. The task states how many columns there are and which paragraphs each covers. A column that covers several paragraphs takes the ending of its last paragraph.
  - `lands`: the paragraph comes to rest; its last sentence settles what it opened;
  - `holds`: it ends on a held moment, a pause or a suspension, settled but not resolved;
  - `opens`: it ends on something still open that pulls into what follows.
  An ending is a reading, not a measurement, and none is better than another.
- `movement`: at most 320 characters: do the sentences move toward conclusion? Say where they do and where they hold off. Never equate length with pace: a long sentence can rush, and a short one can stop the reader.
- `evidence`: 1–3 quotes for `movement`.
- `handoffs`: 0–3 samples of one sentence handing over to the next.
  - `from` and `to`: one quote each, `{"para": n, "quote": "..."}`, from two sentences side by side in one paragraph, `to` from the sentence directly after the one `from` stands in. Each quote stays inside its own sentence. The host checks that the two sentences are adjacent in that paragraph; a pair that is not is withheld.
  - `carried`: one line of at most 120 characters: what the second sentence takes up (an action, an image, a question, a sound, a pressure).
  - `changes`: one line of at most 120 characters: what it changes (a consequence, a turn, a delay, a break).
  - `effect`: `continues` (it continues attention), `pause` (it holds a purposeful pause), `closure` (it earns closure), or `loses` (it loses the connection, which `changes` explains).
- Describe the movement. Never score the prose, set an ideal sentence length, or suggest an edit.
- `friction`: where this reading strains: stress and sound are one possible reading aloud, not a fact; an ending that reads two ways; a handoff chosen because it is easy to show.
