## Lens: stock and signature (key `"stock"`)

Where the chapter spends the reader's attention: the convention that orients a reader without drawing notice, and the lines only this chapter would write. Familiar is not a fault. Stock is infrastructure: a reader coasts on it, which is what lets a signature beat land.

- `economy`: required. The cognitive economy, as beats you classify.
  - `beats`: 0–40 beats, in chapter order and spread across the whole chapter. A beat is one short quote, `{"para": n, "quote": "...", "tier": "..."}`: a gesture, a line of staging, an image, a turn. Its `tier` is one of:
    - `functional`: functional stock, infrastructure that orients the reader and moves them along without drawing notice;
    - `decorated`: stock with an elevation that draws attention to the convention it dresses;
    - `weighted`: stock carrying weight, a familiar beat asked to hold a peak, a turn or a coda;
    - `signature`: a line only this chapter would write.
  - The host counts the tiers from the beats it finds, so the board shows a share of the beats you classified, never of the chapter. Never give a count, a ratio or a target mix.
  - `note`: at most 320 characters: where the chapter spends attention, in its own terms.
- `signature`: 0–4 signature phrases, the most distinctive first. Each has a `title` of at most 60 characters, a `note` of one line of at most 120 characters saying what makes it the chapter's own, and `evidence`: 1–3 quotes.
- `familiar`: 0–6 familiar phrases: idioms, clichés, stock phrases, and stock carrying weight.
  - `phrase`: at most 60 characters, the words exactly as they stand in the chapter. The host counts the phrase and finds its paragraphs. A phrase the chapter does not hold is withheld, and so is one none of whose quotes holds it.
  - `kind`: `idiom`, `cliche`, `stock` (a stock phrase), or `weighted` (stock carrying weight at a peak).
  - `note`: one line of at most 120 characters: what the phrase does here. Familiar means familiar to the reader, not a fault: a period voice, a character's idiom and a quick orientation are all work.
  - `evidence`: 1–3 quotes, each holding the phrase.
- `airLocks`: 0–3 air-lock passages: closure-complete lines, finished and quotable enough to stand alone. This is a craft signal, not a risk and not an accusation. Each has a `title` of at most 60 characters, a `note` of one line of at most 120 characters (what the closure does in the flow around it), and `evidence`: 1–3 quotes.
- `echoes`: 0–3 aphoristic lines that sound reminiscent of something: a proverb, a maxim, a famous line, a familiar shape of saying.
  - Each has a `title` of at most 60 characters; `reminiscentOf`, at most 60 characters, what it sounds reminiscent of, as a lead ("a proverb's turn"); a `note` of one line of at most 120 characters; and `evidence`: 1–3 quotes.
  - An echo is worth a look, never a finding. Never say that a line is copied, borrowed, plagiarized or taken from anywhere, or that it is likely to be, and never give a percentage or a likelihood. The host checks the quote against the chapter, not the resemblance, and a reading's memory of sources is often wrong. An echo with a percentage is withheld.
- Describe what the beats do. Never advise stripping, upgrading, replacing or protecting anything.
- `friction`: where this reading strains: a beat another reader would tier differently, a voice the reading may be taking for stock (a period idiom, a character's way of talking), an echo that may be the reading's reflex.
