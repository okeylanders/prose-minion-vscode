## Lens: lineage (key `"lineage"`)

Which older stories the chapter stands beside: myths, scriptures, folk tales and well-known books whose shape it shares, and how it stands to each.

{"items":[{"title":"...","source":"...","stances":[{"stance":"matches","note":"..."},{"stance":"subverts","note":"..."}],"claim":"...","evidence":[{"para":4,"quote":"..."}]}],"setAside":[{"title":"...","reason":"..."}],"friction":"..."}

- `items`: 0–4 adjacent stories, the closest first. Zero is a valid answer: "no strong echo" is an honest lineage, and the friction says why.
- `title`: at most 80 characters, the story as it is known ("Orpheus and Eurydice").
- `source`: at most 80 characters, where the story comes from ("Greek myth", "Genesis 3", "Perrault, 1697").
- `stances`: 1–3 ways the chapter stands to the story, each with a different `stance`:
  - `matches`: the chapter shares this part of the story's shape;
  - `departs`: the chapter leaves the story here;
  - `subverts`: the chapter takes up this part of the story and turns it.
  Each has a `note`, one line of at most 160 characters, in the chapter's terms. Only these three words are stances: any other withholds the story.
- `claim`: at most 400 characters: what standing beside this story does in the chapter. `evidence`: 1–3 quotes, where the chapter meets the story.
- `setAside`: required, 0–3 stories you checked and set aside, each with a `title` of at most 80 characters and a `reason` of one line of at most 160 characters ("Two children, not three, and no captor."). Name the reflex readings a descent or a journey invites when the chapter does not bear them out. A story set aside is never also one of `items`.
- Resemblance is not quality and not a target. Never tell the writer to borrow, follow, honor or avoid a story.
- `friction`: where the lineage strains: a famous story that may be the reading's reflex rather than the chapter's, a match that rests on one image, a theology or a moral a story brings with it.
