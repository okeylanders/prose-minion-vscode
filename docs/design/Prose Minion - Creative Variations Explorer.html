Creative Variations Explorer — five takes, one set of invariants

The reusable variation surface. Gesture varies a phrase; Show vs. Tell varies one beat along one axis. This varies a passage on any axis you name — and it is the surface every tool report's "Creative Variations" section should hand off to instead of each report inventing its own interactive lifecycle. Same cheap one-shot rail as Spread 01. Three new arguments: declared invariants the workup must survive, two ways to aim the sampling (an open tail dial or a bound four-menu frame), and a measured distinctness readout that stops the widget from promising diversity it did not deliver.

It's interactive: in §1, open the Explorer from Jill's chip or Widgets. Move the sampling dial, or switch to Bound frame and compose one from the four menus. Generate the workup, keep two, press Compare kept side by side, flip a kept card between prose and direction only, accept an invariant flag, and watch the payload strip spend its ceiling. Commit lands a chip that re-opens the exact draft.

The load-bearing sentence: it is a comparison studio, not an automatic rewrite button. There is no Apply to Draft, the generation cloud is thrown away, and what rides is the direction the writer chose.

The one-shot rail, live

What this frame decides
  • Nothing new on the rail, again. One-shot thread-artifact, chip, clone-and-recommit — Sprint 01 wholesale. Three springs in a row have now added craft surface without adding a lifetime, which is the strongest evidence the widget host was drawn correctly.
  • One explicit model call, one closed schema. Position, name, prose, tradeoff note, applied frame, flags. No generic Markdown-heading parser — the reports taught us that lesson; a widget validates a typed result before it renders a card.
  • The writer's problem is not quality, it's sameness. Jill's opening line names it exactly: four rewrites that are the same rewrite. That's the diagnosis this widget treats, and it's why distinctness — not polish — is the thing measured on screen.
  • A persona may recommend and prefill. It may not generate, select, or commit. Same authority boundary as every other widget, and it matters more here: choosing between five good options is the creative act.
  • Commit is optional but expected. Unlike the Learner, a session that keeps nothing usually means the workup failed — so the empty payload line says so plainly rather than congratulating the writer.
02

The seed — and the invariants that make variation safe

what must survive, what must not change, and what the widget refuses to pretend it knows

Pasted text, no passage contextthe honest label, not a silent downgrade
Decisions
  • Two invariant fields, not one. Must survive is what every variation has to still deliver — fact, character state, emotional truth. Must not change is the hard boundary — POV, tense, plot outcome, a specific line of dialogue. Writers conflate these; the generation cannot afford to.
  • The invariants are the whole reason this is not a rewrite tool. A rewrite button asks "make this better". This asks "what else could carry these exact obligations" — and the obligations are authored by the writer, not inferred by the model.
  • Pasted material is allowed, and labelled. Open-chat use is a real workflow, so the widget takes pasted text — and then says out loud that it cannot check continuity against the pages around it. A silent quality downgrade would be worse than refusing.
  • POV is a constraint, never a variable. The pill is not decoration: the generation is told close third on him, so a variation cannot dip into her head to seem fresh. Every widget in this system has learned this the same way.
  • The passage is editable before generating. A writer trimming the selection to the actual beat is doing the most valuable thing available, and it costs nothing.
03

Two ways to aim the sampling

an open dial for "surprise me", a bound frame for "these four things, on purpose"

Open samplingInteractivethe dial at far tail
Bound frameInteractivefour menus, one composed constraint
Decisions
  • Four named distances, not a temperature slider. Familiar · Adjacent · Tail · Far tail, each with the probability band it targets. Nameable, diffable, speakable in a thread marker — the same conviction behind the Controller's discrete levers and the continuum's five positions.
  • The baseline is always generated. Even at Far tail, card one is the competent obvious fix, labelled as such. If the conventional choice is genuinely best, the writer should be able to see it win rather than take our word that it lost.
  • The dial is verbalized, and the panel says so. Distance becomes instruction language, not a sampling parameter. Temperature moves every token including the ones holding POV, tense and grammar together; naming the target in words moves content choices and leaves structure alone. This is a real finding, and stating the mechanism is how the control stays teachable rather than magical.
  • Bound mode is the answer to "diverse how?" Four menus — pressure, narrative distance, carrier, commitment — compose one frame every variation must obey. Open mode varies everything; bound mode holds three axes still and lets you read what the fourth is actually doing.
  • The frame is restated on every card. A variation you love is a combination you can ask for again — on the next beat, or in a Controller lever. That's the difference between a lucky generation and a craft vocabulary.
  • Three to five, hard. Six is a cloud, two is a coin toss. The count control offers exactly 3–5 and the caption says why.
04

The workup, measured — and compared

named approaches, a tradeoff each, an invariant guard, and a diversity number the widget has to earn

After the model callInteractive2 kept · comparison open · one flag accepted
Decisions
  • Distinctness is measured, not promised. A deterministic pairwise similarity runs over the returned set after generation and prints one number. Any surface that claims to widen creative range must be willing to show when it didn't — otherwise the claim is marketing inside our own product.
  • Collapse has a cheap repair. When two cards are the same idea with the nouns shuffled, the strip names the pair and offers to regenerate only those two. Three good variations survive untouched; the fix costs one small call instead of a full re-roll. (Second frame below.)
  • The invariant guard flags; it never rejects. "Absence as furniture" invents a chair — that is a genuinely good move and a genuinely new fact. So the card carries an amber flag the writer can accept, which promotes it into the payload as canon. Silently dropping the variation would have thrown away the best idea in the set.
  • Every card carries its own tradeoff in gains/costs form, plus a word count. No ranking, no "best" badge, no star. The order is the order they were generated in, and the header says none ranked.
  • Comparison is a first-class surface, not a nice-to-have. Kept variants go side by side with the invariant pinned above them — that is the moment the widget exists for, and it is one click from the cards.
  • Carry mode, inherited from Spread 04. Each kept variant rides as prose (the exact words) or direction only (the abstraction — roughly a third of the characters, and reusable on the next beat). One visible ceiling for both.
  • Copying a variation is allowed; applying it is not. Copy-to-clipboard sits on the card. Writing it into the manuscript stays behind the separately-tracked Workshop Apply to Draft safety design, and this spring does not pre-empt it.
When the sampler collapsesFailure statetwo cards, one idea — named and repairable
Why draw the failure
  • Because it will happen. Tail sampling is not free; some passages have one obvious strong move and the model will find it twice. A widget whose whole promise is difference needs a visible, dignified way to say that one didn't work.
  • The number does the accusing, not the writer. 0.14 distinct, flagged in amber, with the pair named. No one has to squint at two paragraphs to notice they're the same paragraph.
  • Partial regeneration keeps the session's history intact. The three good cards keep their identity, their flags and their kept state. Re-rolling everything would punish the writer for the model's repetition.
  • Cancellation and a visible failure state are promotion gates, alongside the closed schema and the item/character bounds — this frame is what "visible failure state" means in practice.

Reports may keep writing variations in prose — they're good reading. What they should stop doing is each growing a bespoke interactive lifecycle for choosing between them. One widget, one commit shape, five callers. The prompt owners for those reports have to agree which of their existing "Creative Variations" instructions become widget-prefill metadata and which stay report-only prose — that is the last promotion gate, and it is a conversation, not a code change.

Boundaries this keeps
  • Specialised siblings survive. Show vs. Tell is not deleted by the general case — a welded axis buys a named vocabulary, a tradeoff readout and a Controller mapping that a generic surface can't have. The rule: specialise when the axis is teachable, otherwise use this.
  • The Explorer never installs standing behaviour. A frame you loved is not a Controller shift. Promotion-to-standing is the obvious next ask and is explicitly out of the first slice — it needs the Controller's shift marker, not this rail.
  • No comparison history in the first slice. Runs are not stacked, diffed across generations, or scored against each other. One run, one commit. Everything else is a second spring.
  • No live regeneration as you type. The dial and the menus are free and deterministic; only Generate costs anything, and it is always an explicit press.
  • Never a knob on the participant. Variation is a property of the work — no persona identity, lens, mode or behaviour is touched by anything on this surface.

Invariants
  • Never rewrites the writer's text. No insert, no replace, no Apply to Draft in the first slice. Copy is available; writing is the room's job.
  • Only the selection commits — never the whole generation cloud, never the discarded alternatives, never the scaffold.
  • Bounded payload, always — one visible ceiling, counted as it is spent, with direction-only as the pressure valve.
  • Closed, typed schema with item and character bounds, plus cancellation and a drawn failure state. No Markdown-heading parsing.
  • Config, not just output, persists — the full draft by stable id in WorkshopSessionService, so the chip re-opens authoring state rather than a summary.
  • New reserved frame registers with the neutralizer in the same change it ships.
  • Core stays host-agnostic; only the composer mount touches the extension.

Promotion questions, as staked here: the smallest useful slice is seed + invariants + one aimed generation + typed cards + multi-select + comparison + one-shot commit + chip re-open — comparison history, live regeneration, editor apply and durable directives all deferred. The generator's result crosses a closed schema with item, count and character bounds, has cancellation, and has a drawn failure state (§4). Commit payload is specified separately from the exploration result and is bounded and visible. Persona permissions are the existing recommend-and-prefill protocol, host and guest alike. Still open, and worth arguing before this becomes a sprint: whether a bound frame the writer keeps returning to may ever be promoted to a standing Controller directive — and which of the five report prompts hand over their variation instructions as prefill metadata first.
